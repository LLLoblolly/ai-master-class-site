👋 Start Here

New to AI, comfortable with computers, or somewhere in between — here is how to pick your starting point, what you will be able to do, and how long it takes.

The short versionIf you can read this page, you can take this course. Go at your own pace, one lesson at a time. Nothing is timed, nothing is graded, and there are no dumb questions.

Who this course is for

  • Adults of every age who feel behind on technology — including Medicare-age learners who want to use AI with confidence and safety.
  • Curious people and busy parents who keep hearing about AI and want a calm, plain-English place to start.
  • Small business owners, side-hustlers and freelancers who want practical ways to save time and earn income.
  • Family helpers — the person everyone calls when the phone acts up — who want to teach others well.

You do not need any coding, any special equipment beyond a phone or computer with internet, or any paid account to get started.

What you will be able to do after

  • Explain what AI is — and is not — in your own words.
  • Use Claude on your phone and computer to get real help with real tasks.
  • Ask good questions (prompts) and fix an answer that misses the mark.
  • Protect your privacy and know what never to type into an AI tool.
  • Spot AI mistakes, scams, deepfakes and cloned-voice phone calls.
  • Get help with everyday life: letters, email, paperwork, planning, and understanding health and Medicare information.
  • Organize your own documents and notes so AI can help you find things.
  • Create photos, graphics and short videos, and explore ways to earn income with AI as your assistant.

Door 2 self-check: where should I start?

Skip Track 0 if you can do all five of these without help:

  1. Change the text size and screen brightness on your phone.
  2. Install and delete an app.
  3. Take a screenshot and find it afterwards.
  4. Copy text and paste it somewhere else.
  5. Tell the difference between a real text from your bank and a scam text.

Skip Track A only if you already use an AI chat tool (Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini) at least weekly. Even then, read A3 — The Art of Asking AI and A5 — Claude Memory & Personalization; most experienced users learn something there.

Please do not skip Track BSafety & Verification is for everyone — beginners and experts alike. Scams and AI mistakes do not care how tech-savvy you are. It is only three lessons.

Learning paths

A track is a group of lessons on one theme (the sidebar shows them as 0, A, B, C and so on). A path is a suggested combination of tracks for a goal. Pick one — you can always add more later.

⭐ Medicare Quick Start

TRACKS 0 + A + B, then LESSON C3

0 builds phone confidence. A teaches what AI is and how to use Claude. B keeps you safe and teaches you to double-check AI. C3 shows how to use AI to understand Medicare and health-insurance information. This is an educational overview — it does not recommend plans.

12 lessons · about 4–6 hours total

How long will it take?

Most lessons take about 20–30 minutes, including the practice activity. The times above are estimates — go slower if you like. A comfortable pace is one lesson a day, or three a week. Your progress is saved on the device you are using (see the “Mark this lesson complete” button at the bottom of every lesson).

Not sure?Do A1, A2 and A3 first. By the end of those three lessons you will know which door fits you.
