📖 Plain-English Glossary

AI words explained in everyday language. Type in the box to search, or jump to a letter.

ACDGHLMNPSTVW
A
Agent (AI agent)
An AI that can carry out several steps on its own — such as searching, writing and organizing — instead of only answering one question at a time.
AI (artificial intelligence)
Computer software that can do tasks that normally need human thinking, like understanding language, recognizing pictures, or writing text.
Algorithm
A set of step-by-step rules a computer follows to get something done. Your social media feed is chosen by an algorithm.
API
A way for one program to talk to another. You will mostly hear this in the advanced track.
App
A program on your phone, tablet or computer. Claude, the camera and your bank are all apps.
Automation
Setting something up so it happens on its own — for example, a daily summary delivered to your email each morning.
C
Chatbot
A program you talk to by typing or speaking. Claude, ChatGPT and Gemini are AI chatbots.
Claude
The AI assistant made by the company Anthropic. This course uses it for most examples.
Cloud
Computers owned by a company and reached over the internet, where your files or AI conversations may be stored — “someone else’s computer.”
Context
The background you give AI so it can answer well: who you are, what you need, and what you already know.
D
Deepfake
A fake photo, video or audio made by AI to look and sound like a real person. Scammers use them.
Download
Copying a file or app from the internet onto your device.
G
Generative AI
AI that creates new things — text, images, audio or video — instead of only sorting or labeling existing things.
H
Hallucination
When AI states something false as if it were true, including made-up facts, names or sources. This is why you double-check.
L
Large language model (LLM)
The technology behind chatbots like Claude. It learned patterns in language from huge amounts of text and predicts useful words in response to what you type.
M
Machine learning
A way of building software that learns patterns from examples instead of being given every rule by hand.
Memory (AI)
A feature that lets an AI remember details between conversations. You can usually view, edit or turn it off.
Model
The “brain” of an AI tool. Companies release new models with new names; some are faster, others more capable.
Multimodal
AI that can work with more than one kind of input — such as text, photos and voice — at the same time.
N
NotebookLM
A Google tool that answers questions using only the documents and sources you give it.
P
Passkey
A newer, safer way to sign in using your fingerprint, face or phone passcode instead of typing a password.
Password manager
An app that creates and stores strong, unique passwords for you so you only remember one.
Phishing
A fake email, text or website that tries to trick you into giving up passwords, money or personal information.
Privacy settings
Controls that decide what an app can see, store or share about you.
Prompt
What you type or say to an AI to tell it what you want. A clear prompt gets a better answer.
Prompt formula (Role → Task → Context → Constraints → Output)
A simple checklist for good prompts: who the AI should act as, what you want done, the background, any limits, and the format you want back.
S
Scam
A trick to get your money or information. AI makes scams more convincing, so slow down and verify.
Screenshot
A picture of what is on your screen, saved to your device.
Source
Where a piece of information came from. Good answers can show their sources so you can check them.
Spam
Unwanted messages, usually advertising or scams.
Subscription
A plan you pay for on a repeating schedule, like monthly. Many AI tools have free and paid versions.
T
Terminal
A text-only window where you type commands to a computer. It is used in the optional Power User track.
Token
A small piece of text (part of a word) that AI reads and writes. Usage limits are often counted in tokens.
Training data
The huge collection of text, images and other material an AI learned from before you ever used it.
Two-factor authentication (2FA)
A second step when you sign in — such as a code texted to you — so a stolen password alone is not enough.
V
Verification
Checking that something is true by using a trustworthy, independent source.
Voice cloning
AI copying someone’s voice from a short recording. Scammers use it to imitate family members, so many families agree on a private “safe word.”
W
Web search (in AI)
When an AI looks things up on the internet during a conversation instead of relying only on what it already learned.
